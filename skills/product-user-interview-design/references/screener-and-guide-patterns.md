# Screener And Guide Patterns

## Screener Rules

- Recruit for the problem context, not general demographics alone
- Ask about recent behavior, not opinions about hypothetical futures
- Include at least one disqualifier that removes friendly but irrelevant participants

## Example Screener Prompts

- "Have you experienced [problem context] in the last 30 days?"
- "What did you do the last time this happened?"
- "Which of these tools or workarounds have you used?"

## Inclusive Recruitment Criteria

**Why this matters:** Standard screeners correctly recruit for problem context but inadvertently authorize recruiting only the primary-persona demographic. The result is systematic exclusion of the participants who reveal the most significant design weaknesses — users at the edges of ability, language, and context. Homogeneous pools produce findings that overfit to the primary persona and miss barriers that will affect a substantial portion of the real audience.

**Solve for One, Extend to Many:** For each research round on a general-audience product, recruit at least one participant whose situation is more constrained than the primary persona — a user with an accessibility need, an older adult, a non-native speaker, or someone with lower technical proficiency. Design weaknesses that affect these participants almost always affect everyone to a lesser degree; discovering them early is cheaper than post-launch remediation. Important: one constrained participant per round is an exposure heuristic for surfacing issues, not sufficient evidence for segment-level conclusions. If you expect to make claims about a distinct user segment (e.g., "screen reader users can complete this flow"), recruit enough participants within that segment across the round or series — do not generalize from n=1.

**Screener question to add:**
- "Do you use any assistive technology (screen reader, voice control, switch access, or large-text settings) to interact with digital products?" — disqualify only if the product genuinely does not target those users and the study goal is unrelated to accessibility

**Diversity across a research series:**
- Age, language, cultural background, and technical proficiency should vary across rounds, not be held constant at the primary-persona baseline
- Track the aggregate across rounds: if all participants in the last six interviews share the same demographic profile, recruit deliberately outside it in the next round

**When to relax these criteria:** Studies focused on expert practitioners for a specialized tool (e.g., internal developer tooling) can reasonably narrow the pool — document the decision in the research brief so it is a conscious choice, not an oversight.

## Interview Guide Shape

1. Warm-up and role context
2. Recent concrete moment when the problem happened
3. Current alternative and its friction
4. Consequences, workarounds, and decision criteria
5. If needed, light concept reaction at the end

Rules:
- Keep most questions about past behavior
- Leave concept reaction to the end so it does not contaminate problem discovery
- Remove leading questions that imply the "right" answer
