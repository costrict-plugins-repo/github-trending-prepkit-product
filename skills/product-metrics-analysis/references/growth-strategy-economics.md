## Unit Economics Check

Before choosing a growth strategy, understand the cost structure:

**Key metrics:**
- **Cost-to-serve per user** — infrastructure, support, content delivery costs per active user
- **Marginal cost of one more free user** — the incremental cost of adding one user to the free tier
- **Near-zero marginal cost indicators**: digital goods, software, pre-created content, AI-generated feedback (compute cost only)

**Decision input:**
- If marginal cost is near-zero → freemium is structurally viable (each free user costs almost nothing)
- If marginal cost is significant (human tutoring, physical goods, per-user licensing) → freemium needs careful unit economics modeling
- If marginal cost scales linearly with users → freemium can become a cost sink at scale

**Gross margin check:**
- High gross margin (>70%) + low marginal cost = aggressive growth strategy is economically rational
- Low gross margin or high marginal cost = growth must be more targeted; free users are expensive

## Freemium Viability

Decision tree for when freemium accelerates growth vs. becomes a cost sink:

**Freemium accelerates when:**
1. Marginal cost per free user is near-zero
2. The product has a clear wow moment achievable in the free tier
3. Free-to-paid conversion can be driven by product experience (not sales)
4. Network effects or word-of-mouth amplify with more users
5. The free tier does not cannibalize the paid tier's core value

**Freemium sinks when:**
1. Marginal cost per user is significant and scales with usage
2. Value requires extended engagement before becoming apparent
3. Conversion requires human sales touch (enterprise, high-ticket)
4. Free users consume resources without generating referral or conversion value
5. The market is too small for volume-based conversion rates to work

**Hybrid approaches:**
- Time-limited full access (try everything for X days, then downgrade to free tier)
- Feature-limited free (core value is free, premium features are paid)
- Usage-limited free (X sessions/month free, unlimited is paid)
- Choose based on which approach lets users reach the wow moment fastest

## Market Entry KPI Framing

**User-count-first applies when ALL of these conditions are met:**
1. **Net-new market** — brand awareness is low, market penetration is the bottleneck
2. **Low marginal cost** — each additional user costs near-zero to serve
3. **Measurable activation/retention** — you can track whether free users are actually engaging (not just signing up)
4. **Sufficient runway** — you can sustain the growth investment without immediate revenue pressure

**When these conditions hold:**
- Primary KPI: active users (with engagement quality checks)
- Secondary KPI: activation rate, retention, referral coefficient
- Revenue is a trailing indicator, not a leading metric
- Present user growth to investors; raise to monetize later

**Revenue-first applies when:**
- Marginal cost is high (each user costs real money)
- Conversion ceiling is visible (small addressable market)
- Market is saturated (growth requires stealing share, not expanding pie)
- Runway is short (revenue is survival, not a choice)
- You've already proven the activation/retention loop

**Switching signal:** transition from user-first to revenue-first when activation/retention metrics stabilize and the user base is large enough for conversion economics to work.

## Future Equity Mindset

Even under resource constraints, invest in what compounds:

**Equity-building investments (even with limited budget):**
- Brand recognition in target market — be known, even if revenue is low
- Community and word-of-mouth — users who love the product recruit other users
- Product quality and wow moments — every satisfied free user is a future paid user or referrer
- Content and platform that improves with scale — more users = more data = better product

**The mindset:**
- Difficulties exist, and when it's hard, we adapt
- Not just cutting costs — even with little money, do something to maximize future equity
- If you want a foothold in a new market, think about how to get brand recognition with whatever money you've got
- Short-term revenue sacrifice for long-term market position is a valid strategy when marginal cost is low

**Anti-patterns:**
- Cutting investment to zero during tough times (preserves cash but kills future equity)
- Optimizing only for immediate revenue (misses compounding effects of user base growth)
- Ignoring brand building because "we're too small" (brand compounds from day one)

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Two identical test-prep products entering the Taiwan market.

**Product A — Revenue-first KPI:**
- Charges from day one, offers 7-day free trial
- Primary KPI: monthly recurring revenue
- Result after 6 months: 500 paying users, $15K MRR, limited brand awareness
- Challenge: high CAC because every user must be convinced to pay before experiencing value
- Growth ceiling: marketing budget limited, word-of-mouth weak (users don't talk about products they only tried for 7 days)

**Product B — User-count-first KPI:**
- Free Listening & Reading practice with no time limit
- Primary KPI: weekly active users (with engagement quality: sessions completed, scores tracked)
- Revenue KPI: trailing, tracked but not primary
- Result after 6 months: 15,000 active users, 800 paying users, $12K MRR
- Advantage: lower MRR now, but 15,000 users generating word-of-mouth, brand recognition, and a conversion funnel
- 12-month projection: conversion rate improvements on a larger base compound — Product B overtakes Product A in both users AND revenue

**Why Product B wins long-term:**
- Marginal cost per free user ≈ $0 (digital content + AI feedback)
- 15,000 free users cost almost nothing to serve
- Each free user is a potential converter AND a referral source
- Brand recognition grows with user base, reducing future CAC
- The 800 paying users ALSO have higher retention because they converted after experiencing real value (not a time-pressured trial)
